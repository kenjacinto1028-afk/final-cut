# FinalCut v14 — Integrated Filmmaking Overhaul

## Design intent
Transform the loop from "start project, wait, collect receipts" into connected consequences for **creative filmmaking, competitive studio management and a living movie industry**. New decisions and identities are derived from actual films, not scripted guaranteed results. Routine work remains automatic and the A+B design remains subdued and readable.

## v14a — Film DNA
Each new production receives a compact, persistent screenplay hook, two creative strengths, one principal risk, and chosen creative vision. Its 14 most recent causal story milestones connect script acquisition, production choices, premieres, rival response, and later catalog events. Historical v13 films do not get invented backstories.

## v14b — The script marketplace
Three authored/curated pitches refresh every 13 game weeks, not on every page reload. They vary in genre, story subject, strengths, creative outlook, price and potential rival interest. The earliest accessible pitch is free and works with a short-film starter. Writers' partnership bids can reduce fee with greater bidding exposure; outright purchase is more costly but avoids a competing bid. Players may always write an original project or sequel.

## v14c — Production tradeoffs
Each Film-DNA project can encounter one film-specific creative challenge during its shoot, with genuine tradeoffs: protect the story, fund extra work, or favor broader marketability. Production time, direct project costs, hype, quality, creative memory and trust may change. Existing post-production, marketing and release decisions remain, and older saves do not retroactively gain events. Delegation can let the director or commercial team handle the contextual issue automatically.

## v14d — Working partnerships
The game tracks a bounded recent history of director–writer collaborations and a restrained trust score. Repeat teams can develop modest craft advantages or tensions from prior decisions. Full career profiles remain director/writer-focused; actors retain the established credit and recognition systems.

## v14e — Release strategy
A twelve-week view displays grounded rival openings and real market pressure. Feature films may favor wide, targeted, prestige or cautious rollout with different opening and leg tradeoffs. Once early theatrical performance is known, a film can take one bounded follow-up action: increase marketing, focus on a receptive audience, hold the current plan, or leave theaters. Spending is recorded to the project and studio; extra sales are uncertain.

## v14f — Living rival industry
Rival studios are still limited by actual cash, active film pipelines, strategic habits and film performance. Some pursue the same limited scripted opportunities. Acquisitions and release-date clashes create actual dated rivalry incidents rather than generic invented news. Rival classifications come from their film records, using the same evaluation as the player.

## v14g — Studio identity, ambitions, and film second lives
**Dynamic company classifications** are earned and reevaluated quarterly from up to twelve actual films within a five-year window (with fallback when a new studio has too few films). Labels: Developing, Independent Storytelling, Prestige, Commercial Entertainment, Genre/Cult, Star-Driven, Talent Incubator, Television/Rights, or Versatile. Two descriptors highlight genre/story specialization, collaborators, critical quality, festival history, financial consistency and more. Industry standing/trajectory are displayed separately. An identity must remain an evidence-backed candidate for consecutive evaluations before it takes effect.

**Studio ambitions** are optional medium-term objectives: launch emerging talent, make an acclaimed profitable feature, establish a genre foothold, reach overseas regions, build festival reputation, or win a network pickup. Achievements may provide small conditional script-access or relationship opportunities, not free cash.

**Film afterlives** let a completed feature choose limited licensing, prestige retrospective, or fan-led screenings when eligible. They preserve the original box office, incur real expenses or reduce remaining rights payments, and cannot be repeated indefinitely. Historic awards and future licensing windows retain their own accounting and deadlines.

## v14h — Integrated interface
- New Project: three script cards, creative vision, script acquisition and release-position choices.
- Studio Profile: primary earned classification, evidence, standing/trajectory, two specialties, delegation, optional multi-year ambitions and historical changes.
- Production Center and project drawers: hook, creative strengths, risk, dated story timeline, film position, early release response and eligible catalog opportunities.
- Dashboard: quick studio identity/profile access.
- Rivals: upcoming twelve-week release calendar, earned rival identity with supporting evidence.
- The in-game, navigable tutorial includes script choice, Film DNA, ambitions and rival competition.
- Styling extends the existing dark charcoal/muted sage/gold A+B theme; no visual facilities or unnecessary micromanagement.

## Backward compatibility and accounting
- Existing **v13 save format** retained; v14 fields are optional and lazily hydrated.
- Independent save slots and campaign-specific posters continue using the existing IndexedDB and localStorage infrastructure.
- No fabricated historical story, classification proof, earnings, attendance, festival awards or weekly film reports.
- All new feature gross, revenue, publicity, production and rights effects use the original studio financial accounting. Afterlife licensing advances explicitly exchange near-term money for lower later royalties.
- Autoplay delegation and a bounded event rate avoid a queue of repetitive decisions.
- The original source remains in FinalCut, not in Entertainment Tycoon/Studio Syndicate.

## Verification
The dedicated `.github/workflows/v14.yml` runs integrated creative/release/rival/identity/legacy tests, two fifteen-year worlds, all v13.1–v13.9 save/economy/film/awards regressions and Chromium checks for script selection, film narrative, competition, classification, saved sessions, mobile and existing tutorial/save UI. Full career simulations and final tuning notes are recorded separately. No code is merged until every gate passes.

## Planned follow-up tuning
All costs, risk and classification thresholds are tuning values. Monitor 61-year careers for dominance by one strategy, script scarcity, classification frequency, event cadence, rival solvency, major award frequency, late-game cash growth and save size. Do not misrepresent sample tests as exhaustive balance proof.
